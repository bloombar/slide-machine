Some slides cannot be improved at this size, and no amount of rewording gets
there. Say so with "splitProposal" when EITHER of these is true:

- The slide carries SEPARATE IDEAS that each deserve their own development —
  distinct stages, a definition and its worked examples, two positions set
  against each other.
- The slide is OVERLOADED: more than an audience can take in while listening.
  Five or six points where three would land, or a body that has to be read
  rather than glanced at. Length is a real reason on its own — a crowded
  slide is the commonest thing a refine cannot fix.

Give the COMPLETE content of each part, exactly as you would write those
slides, and order them as they should appear; the first replaces this slide.
Split the material between them — do not repeat the same points in every part.

Decide this BEFORE you settle on the single-slide wording, and when it is a
close call, OFFER. The instructor sees the parts and chooses: declining costs
them one click and they keep the refined slide, while staying silent means
they never learn the option was there. An offer is a suggestion, not a change.

Leave "splitProposal" out when the slide is already ONE clear idea at a size
an audience can read at a glance. That is still the answer for most slides,
and a slide that is merely worded poorly is refined, not split. Never propose
more than {{maxSplitParts}} parts, and never a part with no content of its
